Why catalytic converter prices move: Pt, Pd and Rh explained

A converter’s buy price isn’t a fixed sticker. Inside the ceramic is platinum-group metal — and those spots move like a live market. Here’s how that works, and how buyers stay profitable when prices swing.

1. Why metals move the buy price

Catalytic converters clean exhaust using platinum (Pt), palladium (Pd), and rhodium (Rh). Buy prices are tied to how much recoverable metal is in the unit, valued at today’s metal markets — then minus costs and margin.

So when Pt, Pd, or Rh move, the same converter code can be worth more or less than yesterday, even if the stamp didn’t change. A printed list is an anchor. Live metals decide what you can safely pay today.

2. Which metal moves prices the most

On the floor, many buyers feel it in this order:

  1. Palladium — often the biggest mover for gasoline converters (Pd is the workhorse metal in many three-way catalysts).
  2. Rhodium — smaller grams, but expensive; used mainly to cut NOx. When Rh jumps or crashes, quotes move hard.
  3. Platinum — still important, and especially heavy in many diesel systems.

Exact mix depends on the unit (gasoline vs diesel, OEM vs aftermarket, condition). You don’t need a lab every time — you need to know that metal mix + today’s spots drive the number.

3. The expensive mistake: ignore the market

Most common money loss: buying on a high market and selling on a low one — or holding converters through a drop. Metals don’t care that you paid more last week.

Habit: check spots at least every Monday or Friday. Before a big load, check the same day.

4. List price vs today’s price

Think of it like this:

  • List / catalog price = a reference for that code.
  • Today’s buy price = that reference adjusted for current Pt · Pd · Rh, then cut for condition and your margin.

New buyers often treat the screen like a fixed retail tag. Pros treat it like a live quote that can reverse by tomorrow.

5. Always leave a margin “safe spot”

Personal rule that keeps buyers alive: when the market is high, don’t pay the full top number. Leave a percentage of margin as a buffer if spots drop suddenly.

That cushion is profit and insurance — not greed.

6. How to talk to your clients

Be straight: metal prices move every day, like a stock market. They’re live. They breathe. They can turn the other way overnight.

You’re not changing the price for no reason — you’re pricing today’s market so both of you stay in business.

7. Holding a load: size multiplies the risk

Holding a small load vs a big load is very different when metals fall.

Example: the same market drop might cost a 50-cat holder about $500, while a 200-cat holder loses about $2,000. Same move — four times the cats, four times the pain.

Don’t hold longer than your margin (and your sell-to buyer) can survive.

8. Quick checklist

  1. Remember: code + metals + condition + margin = buy price
  2. Watch Pd first, then Rh, then Pt (and know diesel often leans Pt)
  3. Check the market Mon or Fri — and before big buys
  4. Never pay “top of market” with zero cushion
  5. Explain live metals to clients like a stock market
  6. Bigger loads = bigger dollar risk on a down day
  7. Don’t sit on inventory hoping metals bounce

FAQ

Why did the same code pay less today?

Metals moved (or condition did). Buy prices follow live Pt · Pd · Rh, not a fixed sticker on the stamp.

Which metal should I watch most?

Often palladium for gasoline cats; rhodium swings hard; platinum matters more on many diesels.

How often should I check?

At least weekly (Monday or Friday). Same day for large loads.
