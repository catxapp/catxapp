How to price a catalytic converter

A clean buy price isn’t just a code lookup. Find the stamp, confirm material is inside, respect today’s metals, and leave margin — or you eat the loss when the market moves.

1. Find the code (canister first)

After you pick up the cat, look for the code on the canister. If there’s no canister stamp, try the shield. Brands stamp differently; shield and canister numbers are often not the same.

Rule: canister first, shield only if the canister has no code. Typing the shield code first is a common way to pull the wrong price.

2. Get a price from the code

Enter the code in your pricing app. Use prices for your region — another country’s list is an easy way to overpay. If only part of the stamp is readable, use partial search instead of guessing missing digits.

3. Check inside — empty cats are the expensive mistake

After the code price, check that catalytic material is actually inside. The most expensive mistake is buying a converter that looks right outside but is empty.

If material is there, estimate how much is left. Material can burn out over time, so the same stamp can be worth less when fill is low. Price the unit you have.

4. Respect live metal prices — and leave margin

Platinum, palladium, and rhodium move every day; many apps update spots hourly. A printed list is an anchor, not a forever price.

A common loss: buy when the market is high, sell when it’s lower. Always buy with margin so there’s room for profit and market swing. Don’t hand over the full screen number with zero cushion.

5. No readable code

If there’s no code (or it’s fully rusted out):

  • Identify the brand when you can
  • Look for similar-looking converters in your app
  • Or use a national average as a cautious reference

Still adjust for condition and margin. Never invent a full stamp just to unlock a high list price.

6. Know your exit before you buy

Don’t buy cats if you don’t already know where you’ll sell the load.

  1. Find your buyer first
  2. Get their pay prices
  3. Pay your clients from those numbers after your margin so profit is clear

Don’t hold converters too long. The market changes every day; sitting on inventory can turn a good buy into a loss.

7. Quick checklist

  1. Canister code first; shield only if needed
  2. Look up the code (right region / right app)
  3. Confirm material is inside; estimate how much is left
  4. Check live metals; apply your margin
  5. No code? Brand + similar units or national average — don’t invent a stamp
  6. Know your sell-to buyer before you stock up
  7. Don’t hold the load longer than you can afford the market move

FAQ

What determines a catalytic converter buy price?

Code (canister first), material inside, live metals, condition, and your margin. Vehicle year alone is not enough.

What is the biggest beginner mistake?

Paying for empty or burned-out units, pricing from the shield stamp before the canister, or using an app priced for another country or region.

Why leave margin if the app already shows a price?

Metals move every day. Margin is the buffer between what you pay and what your buyer pays you.
